# Valtireo Brand and Product Design Foundation v1.0

Last updated: 2026-08-04

## Purpose

This document defines the first brand and product design direction for Valtireo, a configurable Organizational OS by Leading Digitals.

It is not a final visual identity package. It is the strategic foundation that should guide logo exploration, UI design, design tokens, product screens, marketing copy, and future design-system work.

## Source Inputs

- Valtireo product direction and current backend foundation.
- Previous PRD direction: Organizational OS first, Operational OS later.
- Leading Digitals brand guide PDF.
- Arkfilms brand guide PDF as a reference for brand-guide structure and presentation.
- Enterprise and government design-system research, including GOV.UK, Atlassian Design System, Microsoft Fluent 2, SAP Fiori, Salesforce Lightning Design System, and IBM Carbon.

## Product Reality

Valtireo is not just an HRMS. HR is the first entry point, but the long-term product is broader.

Valtireo should be positioned as:

> A configurable Organizational OS for structure, people, workflows, approvals, documents, compliance, internal services, reporting, and intelligence.

The product must work for:

- government agencies
- healthcare institutions
- schools and universities
- NGOs
- manufacturing companies
- professional service firms
- growing private enterprises
- multi-location organizations

The first product line is Organizational OS. Operational OS should remain a later adjacent product line covering inventory, procurement, fleet, facilities, warehouse, logistics, vendors, and field operations.

## Visual Strategy

Valtireo should inherit a subtle relationship to Leading Digitals, but it should have its own product identity.

The Leading Digitals palette uses:

- teal: #65C7C4
- deep blue: #2A5584
- gold: #FFCC29
- pale yellow: #FFF688

These are strong parent-brand colors, but full-strength teal and yellow are too loud for a daily enterprise application. Valtireo should deepen the palette and use the brighter parent colors as accents.

Arkfilms uses:

- deep green: #1B6156
- orange: #FF5200
- dark charcoal: #131A22
- neutral gray: #B2B2B2

Arkfilms is useful for brand-guide structure and confidence, but its orange/cinematic warmth should not become the core Valtireo product style.

## Typography

Recommended UI typeface:

- Inter or Instrument Sans

Recommended brand/display typeface:

- Manrope or Anek Latin/Odia-style display family

Recommendation:

- Use Inter or Instrument Sans for product UI because Valtireo is a data-heavy application.
- Use Manrope for brand headings and marketing because it connects well with the modern geometric feel in the reference guides.
- Avoid overly futuristic, condensed, or decorative fonts. The product must remain legible for daily administrative work.

Type principles:

- Dense but readable.
- Strong hierarchy.
- No oversized hero typography inside dashboards.
- Compact labels for admin screens.
- Use sentence case for UI labels.
- Avoid decorative letter spacing inside product UI.

## Logo Direction

Valtireo should not copy the Leading Digitals mark, but it can share the same family idea: direction, structure, movement, and confidence.

Logo concepts worth exploring:

1. A structured V mark made from connected planes or workflow nodes.
2. A V-shaped operating layer or bracket suggesting organization structure.
3. A modular grid mark where one tile forms a check, path, or signal.
4. A wordmark with a precise custom V and clean lowercase/Title Case construction.

Recommended wordmark:

- Valtireo

Avoid:

- VALTIREO in all caps as the main product wordmark unless the mark is very refined.
- A literal people icon.
- A generic checkmark.
- A gear icon.
- A globe icon.
- A shield-only mark that overpositions the product as security/compliance.

The mark should suggest:

- structure
- visibility
- coordinated movement
- operating layer
- trust

## Product Design Principles

These principles should guide the design system.

### 1. Start With Operational Clarity

Every screen must quickly answer:

- what is this?
- who owns it?
- what is the status?
- what action is needed?
- what changed recently?

### 2. Role-Aware by Default

The product serves different users: Super Admin, Organization Admin, HR Director, HR Officer, Compliance Officer, ICT Admin, Department Head, Supervisor, Employee, and later Payroll/Finance roles.

Navigation, dashboards, actions, and reports should adapt to role.

### 3. Audit-Ready, Not Audit-Heavy

Audit information should be present without making screens feel like legal forms. Use timelines, metadata rows, and activity panels.

### 4. Dense, Not Cluttered

Valtireo users will work with records, approvals, staff files, reports, and exceptions. The UI should support scanning and repeated work.

### 5. Modular, But Familiar

Modules should feel like parts of the same operating system. Do not redesign each module as a separate app.

### 6. Calm Under Complexity

The product should make complex organizations feel manageable. Avoid visual noise, excessive cards, heavy gradients, and decorative illustrations in work areas.

### 7. AI as Assistance, Not Theater

AI features should appear where they reduce admin burden:

- profile summaries
- missing document checks
- report drafting
- policy answers
- anomaly detection
- appraisal or onboarding summaries

Do not make the whole product look like an AI chatbot.

## External Research Takeaways

Enterprise and public-sector design systems point toward a few common truths:

- Start with user needs and operational context.
- Build reusable foundations before trying to cover every pattern.
- Prioritize accessibility, consistency, and role-specific workflows.
- Use patterns for repeatable service tasks.
- Make complex work feel simple without hiding important decisions.
- Design for scale, documentation, and team handoff.

For Valtireo, this means the design system should begin with boring-but-powerful primitives:

- tables
- forms
- statuses
- permissions
- approvals
- activity logs
- dashboards
- document states
- setup workflows

The brand can be beautiful, but the product will win on trust and clarity.

- Microsoft Fluent 2 design principles: https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-principles
- SAP Fiori design principles: https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-web/v1-38/discover/sap-design-system/vision-and-mission/design-principles
- SAP Fiori app design basics: https://experience.sap.com/fiori-design-web/best-practices-for-designing-sap-fiori-apps/
- IBM Carbon Design System: https://carbondesignsystem.com/

Why these matter:

- Atlassian is useful for foundations such as tokens, color, spacing, typography, iconography, elevation, border, and radius.
- GOV.UK is useful for service design discipline, accessibility, common task patterns, and the principle of consistency without unnecessary uniformity.
- Microsoft Fluent 2 is useful for calm, focused product experiences that reduce clutter and work naturally across platforms.
- SAP Fiori is very relevant to Valtireo because it emphasizes role-based, adaptive, simple, coherent enterprise applications.
- IBM Carbon is useful for product design systems that connect brand language, tokens, components, accessibility, and implementation.

Important conclusion from the research:

Valtireo should not begin with decorative brand work. It should begin with brand foundations and product foundations at the same time, because the brand has to survive inside dense enterprise workflows.

## First Review Pass

Initial idea:

- Make Valtireo inherit the Leading Digitals teal/blue palette strongly.

Review:

- This would make the product too close to the parent company and too bright for enterprise daily-use software.

Revised decision:

- Keep a family relationship through teal/blue, but create deeper Valtireo-specific product colors.

Initial idea:

- Use the Arkfilms guide as a visual direction.

Review:

- Arkfilms is cinematic and consumer-facing. Valtireo is operational and institutional.

Revised decision:

- Use Arkfilms as a guide-structure reference only, not a visual identity reference.

Initial idea:

- Make the brand broadly "modern SaaS."

Review:

- Modern SaaS is too generic and crowded. Valtireo needs a stronger enterprise/institutional point of view.

Revised decision:

- Brand around organization infrastructure, clarity, control, and audit-ready operations.
